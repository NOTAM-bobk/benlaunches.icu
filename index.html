Boring Internet — a hand-checked directory of launched websites

Boring Internet is a small, hand-curated directory of websites that have already launched. Every listing is checked by a person before it goes live: the site loads fast, works without forcing you through a waitlist or a sign-up wall, and skips the pop-ups, newsletter traps and growth tactics that make the modern web feel like a maze. What you get is a quiet list of real places you can open, use and judge for yourself right now.

The directory is built for people who are bored of scrolling the same handful of feeds and want to find interesting websites again. Whether you are hunting for a genuinely useful tool, killing five minutes with something new, looking for a website-to-website rabbit hole, or collecting inspiration for a project of your own, the list gives you something worth opening without asking for your email address first.

There are three ways to browse:

  • Start from the homepage, where new and upcoming launches sit next to featured picks and editors’ choices, with a live view of what is trending.
  • Filter by category — tools, games, blogs, art, music, learning and more — to narrow the directory down to what you actually want.
  • Press the bored button and jump from website to website, one random hand-checked site at a time, until something catches your attention.

If you have launched something yourself, you can submit your site to the directory. Submissions are reviewed by hand, and accepted sites get a permanent page with a description, a category and a vote count from readers. There is no fee to be listed, and once your site is in you can add a featured-on badge to your own pages.

The whole thing is kept deliberately small. Pages load in milliseconds, nothing requires an account to browse, and the blog is written by the person who edits the list. A public changelog records every meaningful change to the site.
